# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Keyboard players on a laptop, often arriving from a shared link, with a few minutes to spare. They have never flown a rocket. In the middle of a descent their eyes are on the booster and the pad, not on the edges of the screen, and a run lasts about half a minute, so anything they must read has to be read in a glance.

## Product Purpose

Hoverslam is a browser game where you land a rocket booster on a floating pad by hand, on three levels with a different scenario every run, plus a simulator that explains why an autopilot's settings crashed it. Success is a player who lands one, understands why they didn't land the last one, and wants another go.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, engineered. The tone of a mission-control console and a launch webcast: plain language, real numbers, no hype. Three words: exact, quiet, tense.

## Anti-references

- Arcade rocket games with chunky neon, glow and gradients.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: rounded cards, soft grey shadows, purple accents.
- Corner-only HUDs where every number sits at the edge and nobody reads it.
- Orange as a brand colour in the interface (it belongs to the game's own sunset and flame).

## Design Principles

1. Put the critical number where the eyes already are. Information a player needs mid-descent sits on or beside the booster, not in a corner.
2. Show less, and show it at the right moment. What matters in the fall is not what matters in the last hundred metres; the display follows the phase of flight.
3. Say it with position, not just digits. A dot against a target tick is read faster than a number against a number.
4. Settings stay out of the way. Anything that is not flight data lives behind one button, and opening it pauses the game.
5. One quiet instrument language. Flat black, hairlines, one soft blue for live data, amber and red only when something is wrong.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for WCAG AA contrast on all text. Never rely on colour alone: every state colour is paired with a position, an arrow or a word. Respect reduced motion (no flashing; edge cues fade, never pulse). Everything reachable by keyboard, with a visible focus ring. Touch controls remain for phones.
